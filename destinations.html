Popular Destinations

From neon-lit cities to tranquil mountainous regions, discover where to go.

Tokyo (東京)

Tokyo is the world's most populous metropolis, blending the ultramodern with the traditional. You can witness neon-lit skyscrapers, bustling crosswalks like Shibuya, and tranquil historic spots such as the Meiji Shinto Shrine all in the same day.

  • Must see: Senso-ji Temple, Shibuya Crossing, Tokyo Skytree
  • Best for: Shopping, dining, pop culture, city life

Kyoto (京都)

Formerly the imperial capital of Japan for over a thousand years, Kyoto is the repository of much of the best of Japanese art, culture, and tradition. It is famous for numerous classical Buddhist temples, Shinto shrines, traditional wooden houses, and geisha districts.

  • Must see: Fushimi Inari Shrine, Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion), Arashiyama Bamboo Grove
  • Best for: Temples, shrines, traditional culture, gardens

Osaka (大阪)

Osaka is a large port city and commercial center known for its modern architecture, nightlife, and hearty street food. Often called the "Kitchen of Japan," Osaka offers some of the best culinary experiences in the country.

  • Must see: Osaka Castle, Dotonbori, Universal Studios Japan
  • Best for: Street food, nightlife, friendly locals, entertainment

Mount Fuji (富士山)

Mount Fuji is Japan's highest mountain and an active volcano, famous for its exceptionally symmetrical cone. It is a sacred site and a source of artistic inspiration, surrounded by the beautiful Fuji Five Lakes.

  • Must see: Chureito Pagoda, Lake Kawaguchiko, Hakone
  • Best for: Hiking, photography, hot springs (onsen)

Hokkaido (北海道)

The northernmost of Japan's main islands, Hokkaido is known for its volcanoes, natural hot springs, and ski areas. It's wildly popular for its dramatic winter snows and mild, beautiful summers.

  • Must see: Sapporo, Furano Flower Fields, Niseko Ski Resort
  • Best for: Winter sports, nature, seafood, summer flower fields
